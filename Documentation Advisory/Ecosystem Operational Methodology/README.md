# ECOSYSTEM INVESTIGATION OPERATIONAL STANDARDS

**Version:** 1.0
**Classification:** Operational Methodology
**Status:** Active
**Purpose:** A repeatable, legally defensible standard for investigating hostile online ecosystems as interconnected hierarchies rather than isolated groups.

---

## 1. THE CORE THESIS

Most investigations fail because they treat each hostile group as a standalone entity. They build a profile of one group, publish it, and move on. This produces incomplete intelligence and misses the actual structure of the threat.

The reality is that hostile online groups are almost never standalone. They form **ecosystems** with:

- **Parent entities** — ideological or organizational movements that give rise to multiple child groups
- **Sibling groups** — peers that emerged from the same source or ideology
- **Offshoots** — groups that split, rebranded, or were spawned by an existing group
- **Affiliated networks** — groups that cooperate, share members, or coordinate actions
- **Support structures** — platforms, hosting, tools, and audiences that enable the ecosystem

An investigation that maps one node without mapping the ecosystem produces intelligence that becomes obsolete the moment the group rebrands, splits, or is replaced.

The standard is therefore: **map the ecosystem, not the group.**

---

## 2. WHY ECOSYSTEMS MATTER

### 2.1 Groups Die. Ecosystems Persist.

A single group can be banned, its leadership arrested, its accounts terminated. The ecosystem it belonged to continues. New groups emerge from the same parent, using the same playbook, targeting the same victims.

If an investigation only documents the group, the work is wasted when the group is disrupted.

If an investigation documents the ecosystem, the parent, the offshoots, and the affiliations, the intelligence survives the disruption and can be applied to the next group that emerges.

### 2.2 Foot Soldiers and Leaders Do Different Things

A foot soldier in a harassment network behaves differently than the leadership, which behaves differently than the parent ideological movement that inspires them. An investigation that treats them all as one entity will produce inaccurate conclusions.

The ecosystem model forces the investigator to distinguish:
- Who does the harassment
- Who directs it
- Who inspires it
- Who funds or hosts it
- Who provides cover or legitimacy

Each layer has a different threat profile, a different legal exposure, and a different disruption strategy.

### 2.3 Affiliations Reveal Vulnerability

Groups in an ecosystem depend on each other. A parent provides ideological cover. An affiliated network provides hosting or amplification. A sibling provides members when one group is disrupted.

Mapping these dependencies shows where a single disruption can cascade.

## 3. OPERATIONAL PHASES

The standard is a five-phase process. Each phase has entry criteria, exit criteria, and documentation requirements.

### Phase 1: Discovery

**Goal:** Identify the initial group and its public footprint.

**Entry Criteria:** A target group has been identified, either through direct observation, third-party reporting, or referral.

**Activities:**
- Collect all publicly available material about the group
- Identify the group's public handles, accounts, and platforms
- Document the group's stated purpose, if any
- Note the group's activity history and any public incidents
- Capture and archive primary sources (screenshots with timestamps, archived links, original URLs)

**Exit Criteria:** A preliminary profile exists. The investigator has enough material to identify likely affiliations and possible parent entities.

**Documentation Requirements:**
- All sources linked
- All screenshots timestamped and archived
- No claims without source

---

### Phase 2: Affiliation Mapping

**Goal:** Identify the group's relationships with other groups.

**Entry Criteria:** Phase 1 is complete.

**Activities:**
- Identify groups the target cooperates with, overlaps with, or is publicly associated with
- Distinguish between:
  - **Announced affiliations** (public statements of alliance)
  - **Observed affiliations** (shared members, shared targets, coordinated actions)
  - **Alleged affiliations** (claimed by third parties but not verified)
- For each affiliation, document the evidence and its quality
- Identify any groups the target has split from, absorbed, or been expelled by

**Exit Criteria:** A network map exists showing the target's horizontal relationships.

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Each affiliation labeled by confidence level
- Each affiliation linked to evidence
- "Alleged" labeling for anything not directly verified

---

### Phase 3: Hierarchy Tracing

**Goal:** Identify parent entities, sibling groups, and offshoots.

**Entry Criteria:** Phase 2 is complete.

**Activities:**

**Tracing Upward (Parent Entities):**
- Identify ideological or organizational sources the target draws from
- Look for shared iconography, terminology, recruitment material, or doctrine
- Identify individuals or groups the target references as foundational
- Note any parent that has explicitly spawned or inspired the target

**Tracing Sideways (Sibling Groups):**
- Identify other groups that emerged from the same parent
- Note whether siblings cooperate, compete, or ignore each other
- Document any shared membership or resource pooling

**Tracing Downward (Offshoots):**
- Identify groups that split from, rebranded from, or were spawned by the target
- Note whether offshoots are still active
- Document any public disputes over legitimacy or succession

**Exit Criteria:** A family tree exists. The target's position in the ecosystem is defined relative to parents, siblings, and offshoots.

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Each parent, sibling, and offshoot documented to the same standard as the target
- Each relationship labeled with confidence level
- Each relationship linked to evidence

---

### Phase 4: Translation and Context

**Goal:** Convert raw intelligence into usable, contextual understanding.

**Entry Criteria:** Phase 3 is complete.

**Activities:**

**Behavioral Translation:**
- Translate the actions of each layer into plain description:
  - What the foot soldiers do (harassment, raids, threats)
  - What the leadership does (directing, coordinating, planning)
  - What the parent does (inspiration, recruitment, ideological framing)
- Note where behaviors overlap and where they diverge

**Terminology Translation:**
- Build a glossary of group-specific terms, slang, and codenames
- Note where the same term means different things in different groups
- Note where different terms mean the same thing across groups

**Threat Translation:**
- Describe the threat each layer poses to different audiences (targets, platforms, law enforcement, the public)
- Note which layer is the immediate threat and which is the long-term threat

**Exit Criteria:** A translated, readable intelligence product exists that any reader can understand without specialist knowledge.

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Plain-language description for every layer
- Glossary with sourced definitions
- Threat assessment per layer

---

### Phase 5: Documentation and Publication

**Goal:** Produce a legally defensible, publicly usable archive.

**Entry Criteria:** Phase 4 is complete.

**Activities:**

**Documentation Standards:**
- Every claim must have a linked, publicly accessible source
- Every screenshot must include a capture date and archived link
- Every allegation must be labeled as alleged unless evidence or acknowledgment exists
- No private individuals named. Only public-facing handles and group identities.
- No doxing material: no home addresses, employers, family details
- Clear separation between Verified and Alleged sections

**Publication Standards:**
- Archive must be accessible to journalists, researchers, and law enforcement
- Archive must be navigable without specialist knowledge
- Archive must clearly state it is not a target list and is not a call to action
- Archive must direct reporting to appropriate authorities

**Exit Criteria:** A published archive exists that meets all standards above.

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Legal disclaimer attached
- Reporting pathways documented
- All claims sourced

---

## 4. EVIDENCE STANDARDS

All evidence collected under this standard must meet the following requirements.

### 4.1 Source Requirements

- Every claim must be linked to a publicly accessible source
- Every source must be archived (Wayback Machine, archive.today, or equivalent)
- Every archived link must be included with the claim

### 4.2 Screenshot Requirements

- Screenshots must be captured at the time of observation
- Screenshots must include a timestamp
- Screenshots must be archived immediately
- Screenshots must not be edited beyond cropping and redaction of private information

### 4.3 Attribution Requirements

- Claims of affiliation must be labeled:
  - **Verified** if directly evidenced or publicly acknowledged
  - **Observed** if supported by consistent public behavior
  - **Alleged** if only claimed by third parties

- Claims about individuals must use public-facing handles only
- No claims about private individuals, even if they are suspected members

### 4.4 Prohibited Content

The following must never appear in an ecosystem investigation archive:

- Home addresses
- Place of employment
- Family member details
- Government ID numbers
- Any material obtained through unauthorized access
- Any material that constitutes a call to action against a target
- Any material that names a private person as a target

---

## 5. HIERARCHY TRACING METHODOLOGY

### 5.1 Signals to Look For When Tracing Upward

- Shared iconography between target and possible parent
- Shared terminology or slang
- Direct references by the target to a parent figure or movement
- Recruitment material that cites a parent source
- Shared targets or targeting methodology
- Public statements of inspiration or succession

### 5.2 Signals to Look For When Tracing Sideways

- Shared members between groups
- Shared infrastructure (Discord servers, hosting, forums)
- Coordinated actions or raids
- Public statements of alliance or rivalry
- Shared terminology or branding

### 5.3 Signals to Look For When Tracing Downward

- Public splits or expulsions
- Rebranded accounts or platforms
- Public disputes over group name or legitimacy
- Offshoot groups citing the target as their origin
- Overlapping member rolls between target and possible offshoot

---

## 6. TRANSLATION METHODOLOGY

### 6.1 Behavioral Translation

Each layer of an ecosystem has a distinct behavioral profile. The standard requires these to be documented separately.

**Foot Soldier Layer:**
- Actions: harassment, raids, threats, spam, account creation
- Motivation: attention, belonging, ideological alignment
- Threat Level: high to immediate targets, moderate to platforms

**Leadership Layer:**
- Actions: coordination, planning, resource allocation, public statements
- Motivation: control, notoriety, ideological goals
- Threat Level: high to the ecosystem's targets, high to platform integrity

**Parent Layer:**
- Actions: inspiration, recruitment, ideological framing, cover
- Motivation: expansion of ideology, recruitment of new members
- Threat Level: long-term to society, moderate to immediate targets

### 6.2 Terminology Translation

Every ecosystem develops its own language. The standard requires:

- A glossary of all group-specific terms
- Source for each definition
- Notes where terms are shared across groups
- Notes where terms have different meanings in different groups

### 6.3 Threat Translation

For each layer, document:

- Who is threatened by this layer
- How the threat manifests
- How immediate the threat is
- What disruption would most effectively reduce the threat

---

## 7. LEGAL FRAMEWORK

### 7.1 The Archive Is Not a Target List

The archive documents publicly available material for the purposes of public understanding, research, and law enforcement. It is not a call to action and must never function as one.

### 7.2 Every Claim Is Sourced

No claim without a linked source. No source without an archive. No archive without a timestamp.

### 7.3 No Private Individuals

Only public-facing handles, group identities, and public statements. No private information.

### 7.4 Reporting Pathways

The archive must direct readers who wish to report criminal activity to appropriate authorities, including:

- National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC)
- FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)
- Platform Trust and Safety teams
- Relevant national and international law enforcement

### 7.5 Separation of Verified and Alleged

Verified material and alleged material must be presented in clearly distinct sections. Never present an allegation as a verified fact.

---

## 8. ITERATION AND CONTINUITY

### 8.1 The Archive Is a Living Document

The ecosystem does not stop changing when the archive is published. The archive must be updated as new material becomes available.

### 8.2 Updates Must Follow the Same Standards

All updates must meet the same evidence, sourcing, and legal requirements as the original publication.

### 8.3 Continuity Across Investigations

When investigating a new group in the same ecosystem, reference the existing archive. The new investigation builds on the old one rather than starting from zero.

### 8.4 Succession Tracking

When a group is disrupted, track the successor groups and offshoots. The ecosystem will produce replacements.

---

## 9. SUCCESS CRITERIA

An ecosystem investigation is successful when:

- The target group is documented to the standard
- Its affiliations are mapped with confidence levels
- Its parents, siblings, and offshoots are identified and documented
- The hierarchy is translated into plain language
- The archive is published with all evidence sourced and archived
- The archive is legally defensible
- The archive is useful to journalists, researchers, and law enforcement
- The archive can serve as a foundation for investigating successor groups

An ecosystem investigation is unsuccessful when:

- The target is documented in isolation
- Affiliations are claimed without evidence
- Parents, siblings, or offshoots are ignored
- The archive contains unverified claims presented as fact
- The archive contains prohibited content
- The archive cannot survive legal scrutiny
- The archive becomes obsolete when the target is disrupted

---

## 10. CONCLUSION

The standard exists because hostile online groups do not operate in isolation. They belong to ecosystems. They have parents, siblings, offshoots, and affiliations. They share ideology, members, infrastructure, and targets.

An investigation that maps one node produces intelligence that dies with the node.

An investigation that maps the ecosystem produces intelligence that survives the node and applies to everything that comes after.

The standard is therefore: **map the ecosystem, document it to a defensible standard, translate it for general use, publish it legally, and iterate.**

This is the difference between an investigation that produces a headline and an investigation that produces lasting intelligence.

**END OF STANDARD**
